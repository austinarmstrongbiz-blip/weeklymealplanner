## Saturday — Croissant French Toast Breakfast Sandwiches (makes 12)

### Croissant French toast (the buns)

**Custard** (enough for 12 croissants, 24 halves):
- 8 large eggs
- 1 cup whole milk
- ½ cup heavy cream
- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tsp vanilla
- ½ tsp cinnamon
- ½ tsp kosher salt

The sugar is low on purpose. These are buns for sausage, egg, and cheese, not dessert French toast.

1. Split each croissant in half horizontally with a serrated knife.
2. Whisk the custard in a 9×13 dish until no streaks of egg remain.
3. **Dip quickly: 2–3 seconds per side.** Croissants soak up custard like sponges. If you soak them, they fall apart on the griddle and make soggy buns.
4. Griddle on the **medium-low zone (~325–350°F)** in plenty of butter, cut side down first, for about 2 min until deep golden. Flip and cook 1–2 min more. Too much heat burns the outside before the custard sets.
5. Hold on a sheet pan in a 200°F oven (the Breville works) while you cook the rest.

### Fillings (per sandwich: 1 sausage patty, 1 hash brown, 1 egg, 1 slice cheese)

**Maple-sriracha mayo (optional, recommended):** ½ cup mayo + 2 tbsp maple syrup + 1–2 tbsp sriracha. Austin can add extra sriracha to his.

### Flat-top order of operations

Set up two zones: **medium-high** on one side, **medium-low** on the other.

1. **Hash browns (medium-high, ~12 min):** Oil the griddle well. Lay out 12 frozen patties and cook 5–6 min per side until crisp. Move them to a rack in the warm oven.
2. **Sausage (medium-high, ~7 min):** Divide 2 lb into 12 balls (about 2.5 oz each). Smash them thin with a burger press, a little wider than the croissant, because they shrink. Season with pepper and cook 3 min per side. *(Bacon: cook 24 strips and break them to fit.)* Move to the oven.
3. **French toast croissants (medium-low):** See above. While they cook, scrape the sausage zone clean.
4. **Eggs (medium, ~3 min):** Butter the griddle and crack 12 eggs (egg rings help keep them bun-sized). Break the yolks or leave them jammy. When the whites are almost set, lay a slice of American cheese on each egg, splash a little water nearby, and cover with a dome for 30–60 sec to melt.
5. **Build:** bottom croissant → mayo → hash brown → sausage → egg + cheese → top croissant. Serve with maple syrup on the side.

## Sunday — Roasted Tomato Basil Soup (double batch, ~12 servings)

Based on **Ina Garten's Roasted Tomato Basil Soup** (Food Network), one of the highest-rated tomato soups online. The original serves 6–8. This is doubled for leftovers.

**Pot check:** the doubled batch ends up around 5–6 quarts, so you need an **8-qt or larger** pot.

| Ingredient | Doubled qty |
|---|---|
| Ripe Roma / plum tomatoes, halved lengthwise | 6 lb |
| Olive oil | ¾ cup (½ cup for roasting, ¼ cup for the pot) |
| Kosher salt | 2 tbsp (on the tomatoes) |
| Black pepper, fresh ground | 1 tbsp |
| Yellow onions, chopped | 4 cups (~3 large) |
| Garlic, minced | 12 cloves |
| Butter | 4 tbsp |
| Red pepper flakes | ½ tsp |
| Whole canned plum tomatoes with juice | 2 × 28 oz cans |
| Fresh basil leaves, packed | 8 cups |
| Fresh thyme leaves | 2 tsp |
| Chicken stock (low-sodium) | 2 quarts |
| *Optional:* heavy cream | ½–1 cup at the end |

1. **Roast:** Heat the oven to 400°F. Toss the halved tomatoes with ½ cup olive oil, salt, and pepper. Spread them in one layer, cut side up, across **2 sheet pans**. Roast 45 min until they're soft and caramelized at the edges.
2. **Build the base:** In the big pot over medium heat, cook the onions, garlic, ¼ cup olive oil, butter, and red pepper flakes for about 10 min, until the onions start to brown.
3. **Simmer:** Add the canned tomatoes, basil, thyme, and stock. Then add the roasted tomatoes **with all their pan juices**, scraping the pans. Bring to a boil, then simmer **uncovered for 40 min**.
4. **Blend:** Let the soup cool for about 10 min. Fill the blender **no more than half full**, take the center cap out of the lid, and cover the opening with a folded towel so hot steam can escape. Blend each batch smooth and pour it into a second pot or bowl. Ina uses a food mill for a rustic texture. Blend shorter if you want it chunkier.
5. **Finish:** Return the soup to the pot and taste. Adjust salt, and stir in cream if you want it richer (a common reviewer tweak). Serve hot.

**Make-ahead tip:** This soup is better the next day. Make it **Saturday afternoon**, refrigerate, and just reheat Sunday. That leaves the flat-top free for grilled cheese.

## Sunday — Gruyère-Cheddar Grilled Cheese with Garlic-Herb Parmesan Butter (makes 12)

### Garlic-herb Parmesan compound butter
- 1 cup (2 sticks) butter, softened
- ½ cup finely grated Parmigiano
- 3 garlic cloves, grated on a microplane
- 2 tbsp fresh chives, minced
- 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
- ½ tsp black pepper + a pinch of red pepper flakes

Mash everything together with a fork. You can make it Saturday and refrigerate it, but let it soften for 1 hour before spreading.

### Cheese blend (about 2.5 oz per sandwich, 30 oz total)
- 12 oz Gruyère + 12 oz sharp white cheddar + 6 oz fontina
- **Shred it yourself** and toss the three together. Bagged shredded cheese is coated in anti-caking starch and melts grainy.

### Build & griddle
- 24 slices sourdough, about ½" thick
- ~1 cup more finely grated Parmigiano for the crust

1. Spread compound butter edge to edge on **one side** of every slice.
2. Heat the flat-top to **medium-low (~300–325°F)**. Low heat matters: the cheese needs time to melt before the bread burns.
3. **Parm crust (the upgrade):** Sprinkle about 1 tbsp grated Parm on the griddle for each slice and set the bread on it, butter side down. It fries into a crisp, lacy frico shell.
4. Pile about 2.5 oz of the cheese blend on half the slices. Once the cheese starts to soften, flip the plain slices on top, butter side up.
5. Cover with a dome or sheet pan and cook 3–4 min until deep golden brown. Flip and cook another 3 min covered, until the cheese is fully melted.
6. Rest 1 min, then cut diagonally. Serve with soup for dunking.

**Optional add-ins** (not on the grocery list): caramelized onions, a thin layer of fig jam, or a drizzle of hot honey (honey + red pepper flakes) for Austin.
